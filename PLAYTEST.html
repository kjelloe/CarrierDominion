CARRIER DOMINION

Playtest script · rewritten 2026-08-30 · the Markdown copy is PLAYTEST.md

Two parts. Part one is what I most need to know — six questions, worth more than everything else here put together. Part two is a scenario: one complete war, start to finish, walking every system in the order a real game uses them.

If you only have twenty minutes, do part one. Nothing needs preparation beyond ./run.sh and a browser.

Part one — the six questions

Ranked. If your time runs out, it runs out at the bottom.

1Does the Walrus feel dead?

Launch one (2), wait for it to leave the deck, select it (N), and try to drive it.

It will not answer the throttle: a selected craft sits until you either click the sea to send it somewhere or press T to take the stick. That is the design — the throttle is the carrier's helm unless you are flying something.

You reported it as broken, and I believe the reason was that the PILOT button was cut off the bottom of the button column, so nothing on screen told you the option existed. That is fixed, and PILOT now lights up when a craft is selected and unflown.

So: does it read as obvious now? If it still feels dead, my diagnosis was wrong, and the answer is to put the craft's state on the panel in words — on autopilot versus you have the stick.

2Does fuel biting make you think, or just make you wait?

A full bunker is about an hour of hard steaming; wars run 25–70 minutes. The ship warns at 25% and again at 10%.

Before this change you could steam flat out for an entire war and finish with a fifth left, which made the whole fuel supply chain decorative. But there is a bad version where fuel is simply a tax on going anywhere. If it is a tax, say so and I will put it back — it is one number.

3One tabbed console, or six panels?

J Q Z I each open the console at their own screen; the tabs along the top left do the same with a mouse. The change I am least sure of.

The keys are a radio now: pressing the same key twice closes the console rather than putting it back how it was. Tell me if that fights your hands.

4Which of the three stick inputs do you actually use?

While flying a Manta you can pitch with the arrow keys, the CLIMB/DIVE buttons, or by holding the right mouse button over the view and dragging. If you only ever reach for one, the other two are clutter and should go.

5Is the weather overdone?

Specifically the sea lighting up from inside when the sun is behind a crest. That is the effect that makes water look like water, and the easiest of the five to have overcooked. Rain, the wet deck, spray and the sunbeams are the others.

This needs Modern · High — see Before you start.

6Does anything read as the game cheating?

A storm shortens radar, a rough sea slows the boats, a gale lifts you off the wavetops. All three are meant to be reasons to change your mind, not taxes. If any feels like the game taking something away without telling you, that is a defect in how it is said, and I want to know.

Before you start

Check the tier chip in the top row

It reads something like 1988 · Low or Modern · High.

  • If it is amber, the look you have chosen is asking for more than the graphics tier pays for — and you are seeing none of the weather, the mirror sea or the swell. Click it to change tier.
  • Weather needs High, whichever look you use. Your own ruling.
  • Clicking it reloads the page, but a solo war is saved first and resumed on the way back in, so it is safe mid-war.

Two links worth keeping, once the game is on the host:

https://<host>/?mode=solo&style=retro&graphics=high&islands=16&teams=2&start=0
https://<host>/?mode=solo&style=modern&graphics=high&islands=16&teams=2&start=0

Sharper 1988, and everything the RTX tier pays for. Same war either way — the look never touches the simulation.

Your war saves itself. Close the tab and the menu offers it back. If you ever see “this war is no longer being saved”, tell me — that is the browser's storage quota and I want the number where it bit.

Part two — one complete war, step by step

Start at the menu with 8 islands, an AI commander, a home island each, and the look you want to judge. Each step says what to do and what should happen; anything else is a finding.

A. The bridge

  1. Take the helm. W to build speed, A/D to steer, X for all stop. Compass, throttle, speed and fuel live in the HELM box, bottom left. The throttle bar is clickable — the original was mouse-first.
  2. Look around. C cycles chase → gunsight → birdseye; the camera tabs do the same. O looks astern.
  3. Lay a course. Click open water with nothing selected and the ship sails there itself; the A chip lights while the autopilot has it.
  4. Open the chart. Click PROG, tap three or four points, then LAY COURSE. The button names whose course it is laying — PROG SHIP with nothing selected. CLEAR COURSE throws it away.
  5. RESOURCES on the chart colours the islands by role and shows what is stockpiled where.

B. The air group

  1. Launch a Manta (1). Watch the deck cycle — hangar, lift, ramp, away. About five seconds, and meant to be a procedure rather than a keypress.
  2. Select and fly it. N to select, T to take the stick. The panel becomes the craft's. Pitch three ways (question 4).
  3. WEAPON view. The selector moves to the bottom centre. Pick a weapon and press F, or click the chip. You should be looking out of the aircraft, not at it.
  4. Attack something. Click an enemy contact to order an attack; with nothing selected, a click sends the ship's own laser.
  5. Bring it home. R recalls. Watch it dock — the deck cycle in reverse.

C. Taking an island

  1. Launch a Walrus (2) and send it to a neutral island by clicking the shore.
  2. Deploy the ACCB pod (P) beside the island's command node. That is the capture.
  3. Open the island board by clicking an island you hold. Set its role — resource, factory or defence — and build.
  4. The virus bomb (B) takes an enemy island intact instead of levelling it. Worth doing once for the difference.
  5. Island runways go on resource and defence islands. Land a Manta on one and watch it refuel from the island's own stock.

D. Keeping the war supplied

  1. The quartermaster (Q): island stocks, the depot, the production bias, the refit rows. Buy a refit and watch it build at a factory island.
  2. K makes the nearest island your depot; L calls a supply run. The lighter sails out with fuel, ordnance and parts.
  3. The squadron console (J): the board, the OUTFIT screen (every store has a weight, every hull a budget), the DECK, and SCREEN for the decoy pattern.
  4. The damage board (Z): seven sections, and repair priorities you set yourself.

E. Being shot at

  1. Flares (E) break a missile lock. Y puts the decoy screen out — four inflatable decoys around the ship, at the price of a quarter of your speed.
  2. The Hammerhead: 3 launches the Viewing Drone, and in DRONE view a click fires a missile at what you are looking at.
  3. Take damage on purpose — sail into a defended island's envelope — and watch the damage board and the repair system work.

F. The weather

  1. Run the clock up (. for faster) and watch a front come through. A day is thirty minutes at 1×.
  2. In a squall: rain, a wet deck that darkens and shines and takes a minute to dry, spray at the bow when you are driving into a sea, and the scope's bottom line naming the sea state and what the rain is costing your radar.
  3. Face the sun through a gap in the cloud for the shafts. Not in clear sky, not in a downpour — neither has anything to shape them. Known limit: they are not blocked by islands or by your own hull.

G. Finishing it

  1. Win it. Hold two thirds of the islands, or sink the enemy carrier.
  2. The ending screen shows the scoreboard the fog was hiding, the islands, and how long the war ran.
  3. ESC twice surrenders, if you would rather see that ending.

Also worth a look

  • A second seat. ./run.sh --lan, join from a phone or another machine. The war room keeps the same join code all evening.
  • Watch a war back. ?mode=replay replays the autosaved war through the same engine.
  • A phone. Landscape only — portrait is gated. One scrolling column of buttons a side. I have not been able to measure how fast it runs, so if it is sluggish, tell me what you were doing when it was.
  • The shop window. Sit on the menu and watch the diorama.

How to report

What helps most, in order:

  1. What you were doing — the step number above is ideal.
  2. A screenshot. debugging/shots/ is where mine go.
  3. The seed and the tick, if the DBG strip is open.
  4. Whether it happened twice. Intermittent and consistent are different bugs and I chase them differently.

For anything that feels wrong rather than broken — pacing, difficulty, a control that fights you — say it in your own words. Those have been the most valuable reports so far.
